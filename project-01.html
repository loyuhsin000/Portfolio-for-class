Green Energy Research Center — Renewable Energy Research and Applications

Making renewable energy visible through architecture

LOCATION

Lot 54, Port Section 5215, Wuqi District, Taichung

PROGRAM

Renewable Energy R&D / Offices / Exhibition & Experience

STRATEGY

Visible energy systems, integrated facades, skybridges and landscape exhibition nodes

This project translates often-invisible renewable energy technologies into architectural experiences that visitors can observe, understand and engage with. Located near Taichung Port, the proposal emphasizes wind energy alongside solar and biomass systems.

Research laboratories, offices and public exhibitions are connected by suspended corridors and truss bridges. Outdoor energy installations extend learning into the landscape. Energy self-sufficiency is a design ambition, not a verified performance outcome.

Making Renewable Energy Visible

The proposal aims to bring the public closer to energy technologies. Architecture, facade design, landscape circulation and exhibits translate abstract energy knowledge into tangible spatial experiences. The building acts as a “translator” of energy, allowing visitors to observe and understand renewable energy in everyday settings and consider possibilities for sustainable living.

A Place to Experience Energy

The site is near Taichung Port. Wind power is the primary energy concept, supplemented by solar and biomass applications. Rather than concealing these systems in service areas, the design incorporates them into the facade, galleries and outdoor installations. Energy self-sufficiency is a design objective, not a measured performance outcome.

Layered Spatial Planning

建築規劃三個主要部分：風能、太陽能及生質能的實驗研發空間；供中小企業與政府機構使用的辦公空間；以及向公眾開放的展示與體驗空間。各區由懸吊廊道、桁架空橋與垂直動線串聯，透過錯落量體形成多樣化的空間層次。結構構想採模矩化系統與 H 型鋼構架，回應未來調整與擴充需求。

Landscape Guidance and Public Engagement

Paths from different directions lead visitors through green spaces and open areas, with small-scale wind devices and energy exhibits along the route. These opportunities to pause and observe extend energy education into the landscape. The design also aims to encourage dialogue between industry and the public, presenting a proposed model for sustainable transition in coastal areas.
